Study method

How to study Community Medicine in MBBS

Updated 8 October 2026. Written by Dr. Anshuman Sinha, Bottlegram Health.

Community Medicine in MBBS is the subject most colleges teach from Park's Textbook of Preventive and Social Medicine. You study it by choosing the chapters that become long questions, turning each one into headings you can write in an exam, and checking any number that a government survey can change. You do not study it by starting at page one in the last three weeks.

1. Start from your own university's papers

A national list of "important topics" is a guess about your college. The previous ten years of your university's long questions, short notes, and viva spots are the list that matters. Write that list before you open Park. If a heading has not appeared, it can wait until the headings that have appeared are writable from memory.

2. Read these kinds of chapters before the rest of the book

Teachers and students keep returning to the same blocks of Park, because those blocks produce 10-mark answers. Use them as the first pass, then fill gaps from your paper list:

  • Concept of health and disease
  • Principles of epidemiology, including screening
  • Epidemiology of communicable and noncommunicable disease
  • Demography and family planning
  • Preventive medicine in obstetrics, paediatrics, and geriatrics, and health care of the community
  • Nutrition
  • Environment, occupational health, and disaster management
  • National health programmes and the immunisation schedule

Biostatistics sits beside epidemiology. If formulas do not stick from prose, use a calculator you can reopen, not another reading of the same page. Stroma includes biostatistics tools for that. The topic-by-topic version of this list is PSM topics MBBS exams keep asking.

3. Convert Park into an answer, do not recopy it

A Park paragraph explains. A prof answer lists. For each topic you will actually be asked, write four lines only: the definition, the classification, the uses or the steps, and one programme or one example from India. If you cannot write those four from memory, you are not done with the topic, however many times you have highlighted it.

Short notes exist for that conversion. Stroma has them, with long answers and exam tips, for several chapters.

4. Keep one set of notes

The failure mode in this subject is a new PDF every Sunday: Arpit, then a Marrow printout, then a senior's compiled file, then a Park scan. Each one uses different headings for the same programme, and none of them is finished. Pick one notes set and add the missing heading into it. Stroma is built to be that set on the phone. A legal review book on paper can be that set too. Two unfinished sets are worse than either one.

5. Do not memorise a number from an old photocopy

Definitions in Park change slowly. Survey figures, programme names, and vaccine schedules do not. Before you write a rate or a dose into an answer:

  • National Family Health Survey figures belong to the official NFHS site, rchiips.org/nfhs. Stroma has an NFHS-5 and NFHS-6 comparison in the toolbox.
  • The immunisation schedule belongs to the current Universal Immunisation Programme material on the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare site.

What still has to happen in the department?

Practicals, spots, and family-study vivas still need the exercises the department runs. If a teacher has asked for an answer in the shape Park uses, that answer comes from the book. The comparison with the textbook is in Park textbook notes for MBBS.
