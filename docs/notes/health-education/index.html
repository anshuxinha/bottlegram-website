Health education and communication

8 October 2026. Dr. Anshuman Sinha.

Health education is a process that helps people make decisions about health with information they can use. Communication is the wider act of sending a message and finding out whether it arrived. A poster that nobody understood was communication that failed. It was not health education.

What are the parts of a message?

The usual chain is sender, message, channel, receiver, and feedback. Feedback is the part students drop. Without it you cannot tell a lecture from a broadcast. Noise, or a barrier, can sit on any part of the chain: a noisy room, a word the audience does not use, a picture that means something else in that village, or a sender the audience does not trust.

How is health education different from propaganda?

  • Health education aims at a decision the person makes, and it leaves room for questions. The behaviour, if it changes, is supposed to be understood.
  • Propaganda aims at a belief or a behaviour, and it is content to skip the understanding. The message is built to be accepted, not examined.

A fear poster can be either. If the only content is the fear, it is closer to propaganda. If it also says what to do on Monday morning, and a person can ask a worker about it, it has started to be education.

What do IEC and behaviour change communication add?

Information, education and communication (IEC) is the material and the activities: the pamphlet, the folk play, the clinic talk. Behaviour change communication starts from a behaviour you can name, such as completing the infant immunisation visits, and designs the message around the reason people do not do it. Counselling is the interpersonal end of the same work, one person at a time, with the chance to correct a misunderstanding in the room.

A focus group tells you the words people already use. It does not tell you that the programme worked. That question belongs to evaluation, on the health planning page. The topic sits with the other PSM notes.
