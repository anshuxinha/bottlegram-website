Hospital waste

Biomedical waste management

8 October 2026. Dr. Anshuman Sinha.

Biomedical waste is waste from the diagnosis, treatment, or immunisation of people or animals, and from research that involves those activities. In a Community Medicine answer the marks sit on the colour of the bag, who is at risk, and what happens to the bag after it leaves the ward. The rules in force are the Biomedical Waste Management Rules, 2016, as amended. If a question quotes an older schedule, say which year you are using.

What goes in each bag?

BagWhat belongs thereUsual treatment
YellowAnatomical waste, soiled dressings, microbiology waste, and discarded medicines.Incineration, or deep burial where the rules allow it. Plastics that can be recycled do not go here.
RedContaminated recyclable plastic: tubing, bottles, intravenous sets, and syringes after the needle has been removed.Autoclave or microwave, then recycling. Not incineration.
White, translucentSharps: needles, scalpels, blades. The needle stays on the syringe if you are putting the whole sharp into this container.Autoclave or dry heat, then shredding or mutilation so it cannot be reused.
BlueBroken or contaminated glass, and metallic implants.Disinfection or autoclave, then recycling.

A needle in a yellow bag is the mistake examiners look for. The sharp is the injury. The yellow stream is for waste that will be burned, and a needle survives that well enough to injure the person handling the ash and the residue.

Who gets hurt, and how?

The people at risk are the staff who generate the waste, the people who collect and transport it, and anyone downstream who handles a bag that was sorted wrongly. The injuries that come up in answers are needle-stick injuries, and infections that travel that way, including hepatitis B, hepatitis C, and HIV. Chemical waste and cytotoxic medicines add a second kind of harm that a colour code does not cancel.

General waste from the hospital, the food and the packaging that never touched a patient, is not biomedical waste. Mixing it into a colour bag increases the volume that has to be treated as infected.

What does a 10-mark answer need besides the colours?

Name the rule. Draw the four categories. Say the treatment for one bag, not only the colour. Mention that the occupier of the hospital is responsible for segregation at source, and that a common biomedical waste treatment facility takes waste that the hospital does not treat itself. A spill on the ward is a separate short note: the area is cordoned, the waste is covered, and the staff who clean it are protected. Disaster waste, when a camp suddenly produces far more soiled material than the routine stream, uses the same colours. The volume is what changes.

Screening of the staff after a needle stick is a different chapter. The calculation of how good that test is sits on the screening page. The wider environment topics are in the PSM notes.
