Health and disease

Concept of health and disease

8 October 2026. Dr. Anshuman Sinha.

Health, in the World Health Organization definition of 1948, is a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity. Later WHO writing added that health also includes the ability to lead a socially and economically productive life. In a long answer, write the 1948 sentence first, and add that later clause after it.

What is the difference between disease, illness, and sickness?

  • Disease is an objective dysfunction. Something in the body or mind is altered, whether or not the person has complained.
  • Illness is the person's own sense of being unwell. It can exist without a finding, and a finding can exist without it.
  • Sickness is the social role: the rights and duties other people grant someone who is accepted as unwell.

Public health, in Winslow's formulation, is the organised community effort to prevent disease, prolong life, and promote health. Community medicine is the specialty that does this for a defined population: community diagnosis, epidemiology, administration, and prevention. A community diagnosis names the health problems of that population, using mortality and morbidity, the determinants, and the distribution by age, sex, and social position.

How has the idea of health changed?

The four concepts are easier to remember as a sequence.

  • Biomedical. Health is the absence of disease. The body is a machine and disease is a breakdown, usually explained by a germ. The limit is that it ignores how people live.
  • Ecological. Health is a balance between the person and the environment. Disease is a disturbance of that balance.
  • Psychosocial. Health also includes mental and social function, not only an organ that measures as normal.
  • Holistic. Health is a multidimensional state. This is the idea behind the WHO definition, and behind treating health as a resource for daily life rather than as the point of life. That last phrasing is the Ottawa Charter.

What is the epidemiological triad?

The epidemiological triad is agent, host, and environment. For a noncommunicable disease, the "agent" corner is the exposure or the risk factor. An intervention can change any corner. A vaccine changes the host. A bed net changes the environment. Treatment after infection acts on the agent. Name the corner you are changing.

What does the iceberg of disease show?

The iceberg is the picture of disease in a community. The visible tip is the diagnosed, severe, or dead cases. Under the water is a larger mass of mild, atypical, latent, and undiagnosed disease. Screening exists because of that submerged part. A count that uses only hospital cases undercounts the problem and finds it late.

What are the levels of prevention?

  • Primordial: stop the risk factor from appearing. Tobacco policy before a cohort starts smoking.
  • Primary: stop the disease in people already exposed to the risk. Immunisation, condoms, a healthy diet.
  • Secondary: find disease early and treat it. Screening sits here, because the person already has the early disease.
  • Tertiary: limit disability after disease is established. Rehabilitation, disability limitation.

A screening camp is secondary prevention, because the disease has already started. The worked table is on the page for sensitivity, specificity, and predictive value.

How is a 10-mark answer usually laid out?

The headings that carry the marks are the definition of health; disease, illness, and sickness; the changing concepts; the determinants in one line (biology, environment, behaviour, health services, and the social conditions around them); the triad or the iceberg, whichever the question names; and the levels of prevention with one example each. A history paragraph before the definition uses up space the definition needed.

Indicators, the Ottawa Charter actions, and rate formulas such as infant mortality belong with this chapter too. They are in the app, with the long answers.
