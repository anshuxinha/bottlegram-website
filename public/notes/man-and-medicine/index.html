History of public health

Man and medicine

8 October 2026. Dr. Anshuman Sinha.

Man and medicine is the history chapter. The question is not a list of dates. It is how the idea of keeping a population well changed, and what a health service is supposed to do once that idea is accepted.

How did the idea of public health change?

For a long time, disease was treated as a matter for the person who was already ill. The sanitary awakening treated filth, water, and housing as causes that a town could remove. Bacteriology then made a single germ the explanation for many of those same fevers. Neither story is the whole of Community Medicine. Pettenkofer's argument, against a purely germ theory of cholera, is the teaching example of multifactorial causation: the agent matters, and so do the host and the surroundings. That is the same triad used in concept of health and disease.

What did Winslow include?

Winslow described public health as organised community effort to prevent disease, prolong life, and promote health. The organised activities that answers are expected to name sit under that sentence: sanitation of the environment, control of infection, education of the person in hygiene, organisation of medical and nursing services for early diagnosis and treatment, and the development of the social machinery that gives each person a standard of living adequate for health.

How is this different from clinical medicine?

Clinical medicine starts with a patient who has come, or been brought, for a complaint. Public health starts with a population, including the people who have not come. The unit of diagnosis is the community. The unit of success is a change in that population, not a discharge summary. A student who writes the history chapter as a sequence of famous men, and never makes this distinction, has missed the question that the chapter is for.

The later names, community medicine and preventive and social medicine, are the same subject taught from that population side. Universities still use both labels.
