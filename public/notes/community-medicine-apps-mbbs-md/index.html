MBBS and MD

Park, Marrow, review books, and Stroma

8 October 2026. Dr. Anshuman Sinha.

An MBBS student in Community Medicine is usually trying to turn Park into a prof answer. An MD resident is trying to teach that subject, sit a university exam, and keep survey numbers current. The books and apps below are built for different parts of that work.

What are students using besides the textbook?

On r/indianmedschool, the usual complaint is that Park's Textbook of Preventive and Social Medicine is too long to turn into headings in the last fortnight. The shorter sets named in those threads are Vivek Jain's review book, Arpit's PSM notes, Marrow notes, college compilations, and sometimes Suryakantha. For NEET PG, the same students talk about Marrow, PrepLadder, and short revision notes, with PSM as one subject among many. MD Community Medicine still has its own reading: national programmes, research methods, and teaching.

What each one is for

SourceBuilt forWhere it is the wrong tool
Park's Textbook The reference most MBBS departments still mark against. Also the book an MD resident is expected to know when teaching. A two-week revision, a phone during a posting, or a figure that changed after the edition was printed.
Vivek Jain, Arpit notes, college notes A shorter pass through the undergraduate paper. Students use them to decide which Park chapters to open. A resident's teaching file. A PDF also freezes the day it was saved, and many full-text Park files online are unauthorised copies.
Marrow, PrepLadder, DAMS NEET PG and INI-CET across subjects. PSM is one subject in a large question bank and video set. Public discussion treats Marrow PSM notes as enough for some university papers too. A Community Medicine residency. They are not a departmental library, and they are not organised as the MBBS prof paper of one college.
Stroma Community Medicine only, on iPhone, iPad, and Android. Short notes, long answers, and exam tips for several MBBS chapters. A resident course, an NFHS-5 and NFHS-6 comparison, and biostatistics tools for MD. Any subject other than Community Medicine. It is also not a substitute for the Park edition a department has named.

What should an MBBS student keep open?

Keep the edition of Park the college named. Use one shorter set to choose chapters and to draft headings. Stroma is that shorter set on a phone, with notes that can be updated between editions. Vivek Jain or a finished college notebook is the paper version of the same idea. Marrow is a reasonable PSM pass if you are already using it for the entrance exam. Opening all four in the last month makes the subject longer.

Which chapters carry long questions is in how to study Community Medicine. A closer look at the textbook is in Park textbook notes.

What should an MD resident keep open?

An entrance-app notes file will not carry a residency. Programme updates, a way to check NFHS figures, and material you can teach from still have to come from somewhere. The resident course in Stroma is aimed at that reading. The original orders and survey reports still need to be read. Worked examples on this site, such as the Pearl index and the screening table, are the same kind of note.

Does an MD resident still need Park?

For the teaching and the syllabus that still cite it, yes. For day-to-day revision, a shorter file is what actually gets opened.

Is Stroma a Marrow alternative?

No. Marrow covers the entrance exam across subjects. Stroma covers Community Medicine for MBBS and for MD. Surgery questions are not in it. It also does not map itself chapter by chapter onto one edition of Park. Several chapters have short notes and long answers.
