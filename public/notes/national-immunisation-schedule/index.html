Universal Immunisation Programme

National immunisation schedule

8 October 2026. Dr. Anshuman Sinha.

The National Immunisation Schedule is the list of vaccines the Universal Immunisation Programme gives free to pregnant women and children in India. The table below follows the citizen note, General Information on Routine Vaccines Under the Universal Immunization Programme. The same programme is described on U-WIN and by the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare. A handbook from a few years ago will still say measles rather than measles-rubella, and it may omit pneumococcal vaccine and the third fractional polio dose. Use the citizen note if the two disagree.

This page is the government schedule. The Indian Academy of Pediatrics schedule, used in private practice, adds vaccines that are not in this table, including typhoid, influenza, and hepatitis A. An exam that says "National Immunisation Schedule" wants the government list.

What is given, and when?

AgeVaccines on the citizen chart
BirthBCG. Oral polio vaccine, zero dose. Hepatitis B birth dose.
6 weeksOral polio, first dose. Pentavalent, first dose. Rotavirus, first dose. Fractional inactivated polio vaccine, first dose. Pneumococcal conjugate vaccine, first dose.
10 weeksOral polio, second dose. Pentavalent, second dose. Rotavirus, second dose.
14 weeksOral polio, third dose. Pentavalent, third dose. Rotavirus, third dose. Fractional inactivated polio, second dose. Pneumococcal conjugate vaccine, second dose.
9 to 11 monthsMeasles-rubella, first dose. Pneumococcal booster. Fractional inactivated polio, third dose. Japanese encephalitis, first dose, in endemic districts only.
16 to 23 monthsMeasles-rubella, second dose. DPT booster. Oral polio booster. Japanese encephalitis, second dose, in endemic districts only.
5 to 6 yearsDPT, second booster.
10 yearsTetanus and adult diphtheria (Td).
14 yearsHuman papillomavirus vaccine.
16 yearsTetanus and adult diphtheria (Td).
PregnancyTetanus and adult diphtheria: dose 1, dose 2, or a booster. The note says one dose if the woman was vaccinated within the previous 3 years.

Pentavalent means five antigens in one injection: diphtheria, pertussis, tetanus, hepatitis B, and Haemophilus influenzae type b. The three infant doses of hepatitis B, Hib, and DPT are inside that injection. They are not a second set of syringes.

Which rows do answers usually miss?

  • Fractional inactivated polio vaccine is three doses on this chart: 6 weeks, 14 weeks, and 9 to 11 months. Older notes stop at two.
  • Pneumococcal vaccine is two primary doses, at 6 and 14 weeks, and a booster at 9 to 11 months. There is no dose at 10 weeks.
  • Japanese encephalitis is only in endemic districts. Writing it as a nationwide birth vaccine is wrong.
  • Measles-rubella has replaced single measles antigen on this chart. It does not contain mumps.
  • Human papillomavirus vaccine is a row at 14 years. The citizen note does not state a dose or a brand. Do not invent one. Campaign circulars can change the operational detail.

Vitamin A is often revised next to the 9-month visit in older teaching charts. It is not a row in this citizen vaccine table, so it is not listed above. If a question asks for vitamin A, answer from the nutrition programme, and say that you have separated it from the vaccine table.

The programme list this belongs to is in the PSM notes.
