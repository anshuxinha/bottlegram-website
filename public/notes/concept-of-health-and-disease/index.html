Concept of health and disease

Updated 8 October 2026. Written by Dr. Anshuman Sinha. This is the exam core of the same chapter in the Stroma library, not the whole chapter.

A long question on the concept of health wants five blocks: a definition you can quote, the difference between disease, illness, and sickness, how the idea of health changed, one model of causation, and the levels of prevention. MBBS papers stop there. MD vivas ask you to use the same blocks on a programme or a community diagnosis. Learn the blocks once.

Definitions worth writing from memory

The World Health Organization's 1948 definition is the line examiners expect first: health is a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity. Later WHO writing added that health includes the ability to lead a socially and economically productive life. Quote the 1948 sentence, then add that clause. Do not replace the 1948 sentence with the clause.

Then separate three words students collapse into one:

  • Disease is an objective dysfunction. Something in the body or mind is altered, whether or not the person has complained.
  • Illness is the person's own sense of being unwell. It can exist without a finding, and a finding can exist without it.
  • Sickness is the social role: the rights and duties other people grant someone who is accepted as unwell.

Public health, in Winslow's formulation, is organised community effort to prevent disease, prolong life, and promote health. Community medicine is the medical specialty that does that work for a defined population: community diagnosis, epidemiology, administration, and prevention. Community diagnosis means naming the health problems of that population with mortality and morbidity, the determinants, and the distribution by age, sex, and social position. An MD answer that never names a population is still an MBBS answer.

Four concepts of health

Write them as a sequence, not as four unrelated definitions.

  • Biomedical. Health is the absence of disease. The body is a machine and disease is a breakdown, usually explained by a germ. The limit is that it ignores how people live.
  • Ecological. Health is a balance between the person and the environment. Disease is a disturbance of that balance.
  • Psychosocial. Health also includes mental and social function, not only an organ that measures as normal.
  • Holistic. Health is a multidimensional state. This is the idea behind the WHO definition, and behind treating health as a resource for daily life rather than as the point of life. That last phrasing is the Ottawa Charter.

Triad, iceberg, prevention

The epidemiological triad is agent, host, and environment. For a noncommunicable disease, rewrite "agent" as the exposure or the risk factor, or the answer looks as if you only know infections. An intervention can attack any corner. A vaccine changes the host. A bed net changes the environment. A drug treats the agent after infection. Say which corner you are changing.

The iceberg is the picture of disease in a community: a small visible tip of diagnosed, severe, or dead cases, and a larger submerged mass of mild, atypical, latent, and undiagnosed disease. Screening exists because of the submerged part. If you only count hospital cases, you undercount the problem and you intervene too late.

Levels of prevention, in the order examiners want:

  • Primordial: stop the risk factor from appearing. Tobacco policy before a cohort starts smoking.
  • Primary: stop the disease in people already exposed to the risk. Immunisation, condoms, a healthy diet.
  • Secondary: find disease early and treat it. Screening sits here, because the person already has the early disease.
  • Tertiary: limit disability after disease is established. Rehabilitation, disability limitation.

A common miss is calling a screening camp primary prevention. It is secondary. The worked numbers for a screening test are in sensitivity, specificity, and predictive value.

How to write the 10-mark answer

Use these headings and stop: definition of health; disease, illness, sickness; changing concepts; determinants in one line (biology, environment, behaviour, health services, and the social conditions around them); triad or iceberg, whichever the question names; levels of prevention with one example each. A paragraph of history before the definition wastes the marks.
