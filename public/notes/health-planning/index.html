Planning and management

Health planning

8 October 2026. Dr. Anshuman Sinha.

Health planning is deciding, in advance, how a health service will use limited resources to change a stated health problem. Management is what happens after that decision: putting people and money on the work, and noticing when the work has left the plan. A Community Medicine long answer usually wants the cycle, and one clean difference between monitoring and evaluation.

What is the health planning cycle?

  1. Look at the situation: who is ill, where, and with what the services already do.
  2. Set objectives and targets. A goal is the direction. A target is a number by a date.
  3. See what resources you actually have, and what you would have to add.
  4. Choose priorities. You cannot fund every objective in the same year.
  5. Write the plan, then programme it into activities, places, and months.
  6. Implement.
  7. Monitor while the work is running.
  8. Evaluate whether the objective was met, and feed that into the next look at the situation.

The last step is why it is drawn as a cycle. A plan that is never evaluated is a document.

How is monitoring different from evaluation?

Monitoring watches the work while it is happening. Did the sessions occur, were the vaccines in the fridge, did the worker visit. Evaluation asks whether the objective moved: fewer cases, a higher coverage, a change you said you wanted. Monitoring can be done by the people running the programme. Evaluation is more convincing when someone can look at the result without having to defend the work.

Structure, process, and outcome are the three usual boxes. A new labour room is structure. The number of deliveries conducted there is process. Whether mothers and babies survived is outcome. An answer that reports only the new building has not evaluated the programme.

Where do PERT and CPM fit?

Both are ways to see which activities delay the whole job. The programme evaluation and review technique treats the time a task will take as uncertain, and asks for an optimistic, a likely, and a pessimistic estimate. The critical path method treats the time as known, and finds the longest chain of tasks. That chain is the critical path: delay it, and the project finishes late. You do not need a worked network for a short note. You need which one assumes uncertainty.
